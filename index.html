Understanding this result

    This is a screening, not a medical diagnosis. See an eye care professional for a clinical evaluation.
    What can affect my answers?
    • Display calibration. Displays differ in color rendering. The gray-patch check cannot establish accurate color reproduction.
    • Night Shift / True Tone / blue-light filters. These warm the white point and shift hues, especially affecting blue-yellow plates. Turn them off and retake.
    • Ambient lighting. Bright sunlight or warm indoor lighting can wash out the chromatic contrast the test relies on.
    • Screen brightness. Low brightness can make dot boundaries harder to see.
    • Fatigue or fast clicking. Color discrimination is harder when you're tired or rushing. Slow down on retake.
    • Color vision differences. A clinical evaluation can assess color vision; this screening cannot identify the cause of a missed plate.

    About this screening

    I generate 14 colored-dot plates from CIELAB color specifications. Numbers and shapes are inspired by pseudoisochromatic tests such as Ishihara and HRR; these are original generated plates, not scans of their clinical plate sets.

    The code groups plates into red-green, experimental protan/deutan, blue-yellow, and control sets. It checks foreground/background color differences using the project's single-plane color-vision simulations and CIEDE2000. These checks measure behavior of the mathematical model, not what a person will see.

    The Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999) paper describes red-green display simulations. This project's tritan matrix is an approximation and is not validated by that paper. Passing the verifier does not establish clinical accuracy for any plate group.

    The control plate uses brightness contrast. If it is missed, or the gray-patch check is skipped after a miss, the result is inconclusive. Other results summarize answer patterns using experimental rules.

    Limitations: Sensitivity, specificity, subtype accuracy, and severity assessment have not been established for this screening. Display colors, lighting, viewing distance, number recognition, and repeated exposure can affect answers. The gray-patch check does not calibrate colors. Matching every plate does not rule out a color vision difference. For a clinical assessment, see an eye care professional.
